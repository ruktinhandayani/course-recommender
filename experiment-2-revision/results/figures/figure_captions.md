**Figure 1. Strict evaluation costs the neural models 3.8 to 4.8 Hit@10 points and the Markov baseline 32.** (a) Hit@10 on 4,553 test users (default protocol, open circles) and 4,551 (strict protocol, filled circles) for the non-neural baselines and the six neural models after budget-matched hyperparameter search (E7b); large markers are means of ten seeds (42, 101, 202, 7, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 144), small dots are single seeds, and the right-hand column gives strict minus default in percentage points. Popularity and the Markov models are deterministic. (b, c) Hit@k for k = 1 to 10 under the default and strict protocols; bands span the lowest and highest seed. TA, the paper's temporal-attention pooling over the recurrent or self-attention states.

**Figure 2. After equal tuning the backbones are equivalent on the default protocol, SASRec leads on the strict protocol, and temporal attention adds no reliable gain.** Paired difference in Hit@10 (first-named model minus second-named, percentage points) with 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples of test users; per-user outcomes averaged over ten seeds). All models are the budget-matched E7b models; the last block compares the E7b models with their E7 counterparts, which were selected on default-protocol validation Hit@10 only. Filled markers: Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 within each protocol and user subset (24 comparisons in the family); open markers: not significant. The orange series removes the 1,242 cohort users (27.3%) whose training history is the fixed curriculum 46→47→21. Key contrasts, all users: SASRec minus LSTM −0.06 [−0.37, +0.25] (default) and +0.75 [+0.35, +1.16] (strict; Holm p = 0.004); SASRec minus GRU4Rec +0.89 [+0.49, +1.29] (strict); temporal attention, default: LSTM −0.13, GRU4Rec +0.17, SASRec −0.07; strict: LSTM +0.28, GRU4Rec −0.15, SASRec −0.28 (significant after Holm correction: 0 of 6); selection objective, SASRec strict +0.56 [+0.33, +0.79].

**Figure 3. A fixed curriculum inflates every model's score; excluding it separates the models, and it explains the one seed that failed.** The cohort is the 1,242 of 4,553 test users (27.3%) whose training history is exactly 46→47→21, the first three steps of the curriculum 46→47→21→52→60. (a, b) Hit@10 for cohort users (diamonds), all other users (circles) and everybody (ticks) for the baselines and the six tuned neural models, under the default and strict protocols; the cohort is 1.000 for SASRec under the default protocol, whereas non-cohort Hit@10 spans 0.898–0.902 across the neural models. Under the strict protocol the Markov models score 0.0016 on the cohort because course 52, the validation target, is missing from their history. (c) Strict-protocol Hit@10 of the SASRec + features model of E4 (not part of the tuned set) for each training seed. In seed 89 the cohort score falls to 0.0008 (other seeds 0.9992–1.0000) while non-cohort users are unchanged (0.8513); the all-user mean therefore drops to 0.619. Course-only SASRec (E4) has cohort Hit@10 of at least 0.9992 in every seed.

**Figure 4. The learned attention is diffuse and shows little preference for the most recent course.** Diagnostics of the learned attention over the user's course history for the three tuned models that use it, computed on the test users with at least two prior courses (default protocol). (a) Entropy of the attention distribution divided by the entropy of a uniform distribution (1 = uniform), one dot per seed, bar = mean over seeds: LSTM + attention (paper) 0.93, GRU4Rec + TA 0.92, SASRec + TA 0.91. (b) Weight on the most recent course divided by the uniform weight 1/n: LSTM + attention (paper) 1.16, GRU4Rec + TA 1.02, SASRec + TA 1.09. (c) Mean weight on the most recent course by history length against the uniform weight; history lengths with fewer than 30 users per seed are omitted.

Dots are single seeds (3–5 per variant; seeds 7, 13, 42, 101, 202), bars are means, the dashed line is the base model (0.5142, SD 0.0045).
